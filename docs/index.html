Migration roadmap

Ordered by dependency, not just severity. Key exchange comes before signatures because harvest-now-decrypt-later is the only exposure that cannot be repaired after the fact.

01

Stop the bleeding

0–3 months

Remove primitives that are broken *today*, independent of quantum computing.

29 items ~50 engineer-days RFC 8996NIST SP 800-131A Rev.2
02

Inventory and crypto-agility

0–6 months

Produce a maintained CBOM, centralise algorithm policy, and make the stack able to swap primitives without a rewrite.

2 items ~1 engineer-days CycloneDX 1.6 CBOMNIST IR 8547 (draft)
03

Hybrid key exchange

3–12 months

Enable X25519MLKEM768 on every internet-facing terminator and SSH bastion. This closes harvest-now-decrypt-later first, because it is the only exposure that cannot be undone later.

2 items ~2 engineer-days FIPS 203draft-ietf-tls-ecdhe-mlkemOpenSSH 9.9+

Post-quantum signatures

Method

Everything in this report is reproducible from the command line. The scoring model is documented, versioned, and has no hidden weights.

The Mosca inequality

Mosca's theorem says a system is quantum-safe only when the sum of the time data must stay confidential and the time needed to migrate is shorter than the time until a cryptographically relevant quantum computer exists.

exposed ⇔ shelfLife + migrationLeadTime > yearsToCRQC # shelfLife years the data must stay secret # migrationLeadTime derived from how widely the primitive is used # yearsToCRQC configurable planning assumption (default 2033)

The score

Each finding is scored 0–100 as a weighted sum of quantum vulnerability (0.40), data durability (0.20), breadth of use (0.15), classical weakness (0.15) and exposure surface (0.10).
